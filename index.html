    How conversions work

    • Coordinates are handled internally as WGS84 latitude/longitude. Shapefiles are reprojected using their .prj file, or the source coordinate system you pick. With no .prj, UTM-looking numbers are read as UTM zone 11N, WGS 84.
    • CSV input needs latitude and longitude columns, found by name (lat, latitude, lon, long, lng, x, y) or chosen from the lists. Degrees-minutes such as 32°42.69'N work too. A WKT column, if present, is used for lines and polygons.
    • folder is the path of KML Documents and Folders above each feature. GPX items get Waypoints, Routes or Tracks. KML output rebuilds the folders from it.
    • Attribute fields come from KML ExtendedData, two-column tables in descriptions, GPX comment, symbol and type, GeoJSON properties and extra CSV columns. Untick a column in the preview to leave it out of any format.
    • GPX has no polygons or attribute table. Polygon outlines become closed tracks, and attributes go into each item's comment.
    • KML output uses standard colours. Original KML styles and icons are not carried over.
    • length_m and area_m2 are geodesic estimates. Polygon holes are subtracted from area.
    • The map offers satellite, ocean and street basemaps from Esri and OpenStreetMap. Loading tiles tells those services which area you are viewing, but your file contents never leave the browser. Without internet the page falls back to a plain plot.
    • Shapefile output is a zip. Each shapefile holds one geometry type, so mixed data becomes separate point, line and polygon shapefiles. Field names are cut to 10 characters, text to 254 characters, and attributes are UTF-8 (with a .cpg file).
    • Not converted: KML network links, image overlays, 3D models and multipatch shapefiles.
